# Candidate C — Spendmesh

## Concept

Spendmesh is a cloud cost / spend-allocation console for a platform/FinOps team: its home view answers "where is our cloud spend actually going, by team and by service" without a single pre-built report. The macro-bucket is the assigned rail-less, full-bleed single dominant visualization — a two-level squarified treemap (team → service → cost) fills nearly the entire viewport below a slim top bar (brand lockup, workspace switcher, horizontal nav tabs, ⌘K search, "New alert" CTA, notifications, account menu — no persistent left sidebar at any width) and a thin inline-stats KPI strip (one hero total + four compact label/value pairs + the period segmented control, deliberately not a four-card row). Clicking a tile opens an ephemeral selection drawer (slides in from the right, with its own cell's cost, share-of-team, share-of-workspace, a 3-month trend sparkline and sibling-category breakdown) that is only ever mounted while something is selected — `<AnimatePresence>` renders nothing to the DOM at rest, so there is no hidden/empty detail pane waiting to be filled. Five real, independently-scoped interactions ship: ① clicking (or Enter/Space-activating) a treemap tile opens the drawer with that cell's full breakdown ② a segmented control toggles between the treemap and the required Hierarchical/Nested fallback — a collapsible, indented team→service table with its own real column sort (`aria-sort` on Name/Cost) and its own text filter, reachable from the view toggle, independently of whatever is selected ③ the period segmented control (This month / Last month / Quarter) recomputes the treemap's actual tile sizes and the table's rows from three hand-authored monthly snapshots, one of which (quarter) is mechanically summed from the other two so it can never drift ④ every tile has a real hover/focus tooltip (one shared element positioned off the active tile's measured rect, not one node per tile) that adds share-of-parent and a period-over-period delta — strictly additional detail, since the headline category + formatted cost is already permanent text on every tile above a floor size ⑤ a "View this row in the table" button inside the drawer switches the view and scroll-highlights the matching row, deliberately a one-way convenience bridge rather than a structural coupling between the view toggle and the selection state. Below 640px the treemap option is hidden and the table becomes the only view, since the smallest category tiles would otherwise render under the 24px target-size floor. Typography is Pretendard-only end to end — no display-font variable is used anywhere, including the page's own `<h1>` — with exactly three rendered weights (400/500/600), tabular-nums on every currency/percent/count, and an 11px uppercase-tracked label convention throughout. The accent is standard Tailwind `violet-500/600/700` (never a custom hex), used only for focus rings, the selected tile's border, the primary CTA, the active segmented pill and one progress bar — every treemap tile itself stays on a desaturated zinc/slate scale, colored by category as a coarse visual grouping, never as the sole signal (every tile also carries a permanent text label). Against Mercury/Asana/n8n/Coinbase-level references, the completeness bar this aims for is: a slim, consistently-44px-control top bar with working notification/account menus and a real ⌘K jump search; a component system (Card, segmented control, category badge, progress, sparkline) shared identically between the treemap and its fallback table; a semantic, sortable, `caption`/`scope`-correct table; and a selection drawer with real focus-trap/return-focus/body-scroll-lock behavior shared via one small utility so two overlays can never silently fight over the same lock during an exit/enter transition overlap.

## 브리프에 없던 것

1. ① How many teams/service-categories to model, and whether every team needs every category.
   ② Six teams (Platform Engineering, Payments, Growth & Marketing, Data & ML, Mobile, Core API) over six service categories (Compute, Database, Storage, Network & CDN, Observability, Other), with each team carrying 4–5 of the six — Platform Engineering is the only team with all five non-"Other" categories, which gives the treemap's largest group block the richest internal subdivision.
   ③ Arbitrary, chosen to land the grand total in a believable mid-size-company monthly cloud bill (~$1.4–1.5M/mo) with no team or category ever so small its tile would fall under a safe minimum size at desktop widths.

2. ① A fixed category→fill-color mapping that is simultaneously "near-monochrome," legible with light-on-dark text at every tile size, and visually distinct enough that two categories co-occurring in the *same* team are never confusable.
   ② Four zinc shades (600/700/800/900, all ≥4.5:1 with zinc-50 text) for compute/database/storage/observability, one flipped light tile (zinc-300 fill + zinc-900 text) for network, and a dashed border + icon as the differentiator for "other" (same dark fill as observability, since the two never appear in the same team in this dataset).
   ③ Arbitrary engineering choice once I'd verified by hand that zinc-500 (a natural "5th shade") fails AA with light text (4.29:1) — rather than reach for a second hue family and risk the brief's "near-monochrome" instruction, I solved the 5th/6th-category problem with one lightness-flip and one border treatment instead.

3. ① Whether the KPI strip's "vs prior period" comparison should exist for every period, including "Quarter," which has no older quarter on hand in this dataset.
   ② Only This month and Last month show a real period-over-period delta (against the adjacent real month in the data); Quarter shows "Avg per month" instead of fabricating a prior-quarter comparison.
   ③ Arbitrary, but deliberately conservative: the brief requires deterministic, reconciled data, and inventing a 4th-and-5th month purely to backfill a quarter-over-quarter delta felt like more fabrication than the KPI strip's own completeness required.

4. ① How a rail-less bucket's mobile nav should work, and how the treemap itself should degrade at narrow widths (the brief left both as the designer's call to document).
   ② Mobile collapses all nav into a slide-in drawer from the top bar (no rail at any width); below 640px the view-mode toggle is replaced by a static "Table view on narrow screens" note and the treemap option is unavailable, because at that width the smallest real category tiles would render under the 24px target-size floor.
   ③ Precedent from this round's own brief text ("switch to table view by default on narrow viewports... your call, document the choice") plus a direct measurement: at ~358px of usable width the smallest category (≈3.4% of its team) would resolve to roughly a 16px tile dimension.

5. ① Whether two ephemeral overlays (e.g. the selection drawer and the search palette) could ever legitimately be mounted at the same time, given `<AnimatePresence>` keeps an exiting overlay mounted for the length of its exit transition.
   ② All four overlays (selection drawer, search, new-alert modal, mobile nav) are made mutually exclusive at the state layer (opening one explicitly closes the others), and in addition share one small module-level "how many overlays want the scroll locked" counter instead of each one independently setting `document.body.style.overflow`, so a brief exit/enter overlap can never have one overlay's cleanup wrongly re-enable page scroll while another is still open.
   ③ Caught by reasoning through the exact mechanics of `<AnimatePresence>` rather than from any prior-art reference — this felt like exactly the kind of "two real but independently-reasonable fixes interact badly" class of bug the brief's delta notes warn about for other rule families, so I built the shared counter preemptively instead of waiting to discover the race.

6. ① Whether the primary "New alert" top-bar action and the secondary nav items (Budgets, Anomalies, Reports, Workspace switcher, Account settings, Sign out) needed real destination pages to be "actually wired," given this is a single-route candidate.
   ② "New alert" opens a real, stateful modal (team select + numeric threshold, both controlled, with a disabled-until-valid submit that produces a toast naming the exact team and threshold entered); the nav placeholders and menu items each fire a real `onClick` that pushes a toast naming themselves ("Budgets isn't wired up in this preview yet") rather than doing nothing or silently navigating nowhere.
   ③ Direct application of the hard rule "every control that looks clickable must actually be wired" — a toast naming itself is a lighter-weight honest alternative to building out five placeholder destination pages for a single-candidate route.
