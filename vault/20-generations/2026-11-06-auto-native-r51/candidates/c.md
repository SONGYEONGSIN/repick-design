# Candidate C — Authenticator Review Queue

A staff-facing screen for the authenticator role — the specialist who verifies whether a submitted item is genuine before a certificate is issued, distinct from every buyer/seller screen in this catalog. It shows a queue of pending authentication cases (priority-tagged, with the seller's declared value, condition notes and photo count), where the authenticator's core action is to tap "Start review" to put a claim-based lock on a case: that lock is granted the instant they take ownership, not once they finish anything, and the row flips to show "Locked to you" with a "Release case" control. Exactly one case can be actively claimed at a time, enforced structurally by a single `claimedCaseId: string | null` rather than a set; trying to start a second case while one is held doesn't silently do anything or pop an Alert — it opens an inline, row-scoped choice ("Keep browsing" vs. "Release & claim this instead") on the case that was just tried, so the conflict is explained and resolved deliberately. A separate case shape — "Locked by {teammate}" — shows cases a fellow authenticator already holds, rendered with no Pressable at all since nothing on this screen can act on them. One live region (`accessibilityLiveRegion="polite"`) sits just under the header and carries every claim/release/conflict transition as an `accessibilityRole="alert"` sentence; releasing a held case flips that row in place into a Cancel/Confirm pair instead of spawning a native dialog.

## 브리프에 없던 것

1. **① "Claim" vs. "Start review" as the visible button label.** ② Used "Start review" as the primary CTA text throughout (internally it performs the claim). ③ The brief offered both ("tap 'Claim' / 'Start review'") as synonyms without picking one; "Start review" reads more naturally for an authenticator persona who is about to inspect an item, so it was the arbitrary pick — the underlying mechanic (claim-based lock) is identical either way.

2. **① How to resolve "already have one claimed" when starting a second case.** ② Blocked with an explanation, not auto-released — but the explanation includes a one-tap "Release & claim this instead" action on the same row, so resolving it is still a single explicit tap rather than a two-screen detour. ③ Brief explicitly allowed either block-with-explanation or auto-release, "your call," but required it be deliberate and stated; auto-release risked silently dropping a case someone was mid-review on, so blocking (with a fast, explicit escape hatch) was the safer arbitrary choice for a production review tool.

3. **① Whether other authenticators' claims should be visible/represented at all.** ② Added a `lockedByOtherName` field on two of the eight dummy cases, rendered as a static "Locked by {name}" chip with no Pressable and no accessibilityHint (nothing happens on tap). ③ The brief's a11y section mentions an "already claimed elsewhere" conflict phrase, implying a multi-authenticator queue is the intended mental model (not just a single-user demo) — invented as a read-only, dead-end row shape rather than guessing at cross-user claim-stealing UI, since the brief never specifies what (if anything) another authenticator's claim should let *you* do.

4. **① Where the live region physically lives, given "only one live region at once."** ② Put exactly one `accessibilityLiveRegion="polite"` container in the screen header (not per-row), and had every row-level transition (claim, release, conflict) funnel its announcement sentence into that single shared container instead of tagging the acted-on row itself as a second live region. ③ The brief requires state-transition announcements *and* forbids more than one live region at once, but the mechanic is row-scoped — a strict per-row live region would mean a second live region could exist briefly whenever two rows are mid-transition. Centralizing into one header-level region was the arbitrary resolution that satisfies both rules unconditionally rather than relying on timing to avoid overlap.

5. **① Dummy data domain/specifics (items, sellers, declared values, wait times).** ② Eight cases spanning watches/footwear/bags/apparel/accessories, authored with fixed `waitedMinutes` integers used only to pre-sort the array (expedited-first, longest-waiting-first within each priority tier), never computed at render time. ③ Brief required determinism (no `Date.now`/`Math.random`) but gave no actual case content; invented a believable authenticator-queue data set and baked the intended sort order directly into the array's literal ordering so no runtime sort logic was needed at all.

6. **① The ₩ / tabular-nums "breathing room" treatment for declared value.** ② Split the glyph and the digits into two sibling `Text` nodes with `marginRight: 3` on the ₩ node, rather than touching `formatWon` or inventing any new structural fix. ③ The brief explicitly warned off a new structural fix for the ₩-reads-as-strikethrough issue and said the screen "likely doesn't hinge on money display anyway" — declared value here is secondary context (not a primary transactional number), so a minimal, purely-spacing, non-structural treatment was the lowest-risk arbitrary choice.
