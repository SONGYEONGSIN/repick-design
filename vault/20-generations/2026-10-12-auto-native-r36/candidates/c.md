# Candidate C — Return Inspection Decision

## Concept
`ReturnInspectionDecisionScreen` is the seller's post-arrival inspection view for a single already-shipped-back return: it surfaces the item's originally listed condition, the buyer's stated return reason and note, and a horizontal strip of the buyer's submitted photos, then lets the seller make one terminal call via a bottom decision band. The band starts as a two-up "Accept Return" / "Reject Return" choice; picking either transforms the same band in place into a choice-specific confirmation (Accept shows the exact refund amount about to be issued; Reject requires a short dispute reason before it can be confirmed), and confirming transforms the band once more into a terminal result (refund-issued or dispute-opened) with no further action available. The whole band lives in one `accessibilityLiveRegion="polite"` container, and every transition writes a transient `accessibilityRole="alert"` line that self-clears after 4 seconds so the alert role never sticks on indefinitely; no native `Alert` is used anywhere.

"Return Inspection"

## 브리프에 없던 것 (What the brief didn't specify)
1. **Where exactly the buyer's photos should go and how many to show.** Decided: a horizontal `FlatList` strip of 3 fixed photo placeholders (swatch1/2/3 from tokens) with short captions, placed between the buyer's stated-reason card and the decision band. Reasoning: the brief said "2-3 photos" and left layout to me; a horizontal strip keeps the photos visually subordinate to the written reason/condition comparison, which is the actual evidence the seller is judging, while still being scannable without consuming vertical space the band needs.
2. **Whether Accept needed a confirm step at all, since only Reject was called "destructive" in the UX-catalog sense.** Decided: give Accept the exact same confirm-before-commit treatment as Reject (showing the precise refund amount before it fires). Reasoning: the brief explicitly says "Accept and Reject are BOTH terminal decisions here... so the same confirm-before-commit principle applies to both, not just Reject" — so skipping Accept's confirm step would violate that line even though it's the "positive" path.
3. **How to validate the required reject reason without a native alert and without silently blocking.** Decided: pressing Confirm Rejection with an empty reason keeps the band in the `confirmReject` phase, sets an inline danger-toned error directly under the field, and also pushes that same message into the transient live-region alert — rather than disabling the Confirm button outright. Reasoning: a disabled-looking button with no feedback is a worse a11y/UX outcome than a clearly-stated inline error near the field (catalog rule: "error feedback near the problem"), and it keeps the hint copy on the field ("Required...") truthfully matching what's enforced.
4. **How the ₩ amount should be formatted given the glyph-crowding note in tokens guidance.** Decided: use "KRW 38,000" notation instead of "₩38,000". Reasoning: the brief flagged the ₩ glyph visually crowding adjacent digits at body size and said to pick one approach and stay consistent; KRW-notation sidesteps the glyph issue entirely without needing an extra inserted space that could look like a typo.
5. **Whether the transient alert text and the permanent result copy should be the same string.** Decided: keep them different — the transient alert ("Return accepted. A refund of KRW 38,000 has been issued...") announces the event once and clears after 4s, while a separate permanent `Text` (no alert role) in the result panel restates the outcome for as long as the screen is open. Reasoning: this satisfies the hard rule that a "just happened" alert must be actively clearable, while still leaving sighted and screen-reader users a durable, non-alert-role record of the decision after the alert has cleared.
6. **Naming the screen distinctly from the existing buyer-side `DisputeCenterScreen`.** Decided: titled the screen "Return Inspection" (file `ReturnInspectionDecisionScreen.tsx`) and framed all copy around inspecting a physically-arrived returned item ("arrived at your return address", listed-condition comparison, buyer's submitted photos) rather than filing/tracking a claim. Reasoning: the brief was explicit that this must read as the seller's decide-what-happens-next view of an already-returned item, not a reskin of the buyer's dispute-filing flow.
