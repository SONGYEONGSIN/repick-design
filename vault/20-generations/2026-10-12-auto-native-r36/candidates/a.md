# Candidate A — Seller Payout Hold Review

## Concept

`SellerPayoutHoldReviewScreen` shows a seller whose payout is frozen (triggered by a newly linked bank account, an open buyer dispute, and an unusual sales-volume spike all landing in the same week) a checklist of exactly the requirements standing between them and their money, and a persistent bottom band that does the narrating: it states in one sentence which specific requirement is still blocking release, and pressing its button scrolls the `FlatList` checklist above (via `scrollToIndex`, with an `onScrollToIndexFailed` offset fallback) to that item and visually spotlights its card. Resolving items in the checklist (each has its own inline action button) re-derives the next blocker and re-narrates the band automatically — nothing is hand-tracked. Once the last requirement resolves, the band does not linger as a dead disabled control; it swaps to a visually distinct terminal band (success-toned, checkmark glyph, no button) confirming the hold is released and funds are on the way.

## Render-check string

"Payout On Hold"

## 브리프에 없던 것 (What the brief didn't specify)

1. **What counts as a "requirement" in a payout-hold domain.** ① The brief named three example triggers (new bank account, sales spike, buyer dispute) but not how many discrete checklist items that implies or whether one trigger maps to one or several items. ② I split it into 5 concrete items: bank verification, dispute response, identity confirmation for the spike, a shipment-tracking item (seeded already-resolved), and a policy acknowledgment. ③ Mixing one pre-resolved item in with four pending ones lets the screen demonstrate the inactive/resolved visual state and partial progress on first mount, rather than only ever showing an all-pending or all-done extreme.

2. **Whether the user can actually resolve items on this screen, or only read about them.** ① "Payout hold review" could be read-only (status mirrors backend state) or interactive (actions happen here). ② I made each pending card's action button actually flip that item to "done" locally. ③ A purely read-only screen can't demonstrate the band's core job — renarrating as the next unmet requirement changes and eventually reaching the terminal state — so the macro-form would be unfalsifiable without some in-screen way to resolve items.

3. **How the band's live announcement should be structured to satisfy the two alert sub-patterns correctly.** ① The brief requires an actively-cleared alert for "just happened" transitions but a safe-harbor (no clearing needed) for continuously-current derived text. ② I split these into two separate UI pieces: a persistent, no-alert-role narration sentence ("Payout can't release yet — …") that is recomputed fresh every render (safe harbor, never stale), and a separate small "cue" `Text` with `accessibilityRole="alert"` inside an `accessibilityLiveRegion="polite"` container that only mounts when a transition cue is set, and is unmounted back to empty by a `setTimeout` ~4.2s later. ③ Conflating the two into one always-alerting node would either spam the alert role on every render (safe-harbor text is literally always "current") or leave a stale alert stuck on after the first transition — exactly the two documented failure modes.

4. **What the bottom band's call-to-action should literally say and do when nothing is wrong yet vs. when everything resolves.** ① Nothing in the brief gives copy for the pending-vs-resolved states. ② Pending: "Show me what's blocking it" (a verb phrase describing the scroll/spotlight action, not a vague "Continue"). Resolved: no button at all, replaced by a success headline + explanation sentence. ③ Keeping a button present but disabled in the resolved state is exactly the "fake disabled button" anti-pattern the brief calls out, so the resolved band structurally removes the Pressable rather than disabling it.

5. **Currency display given the ₩-glyph crowding note.** ① The brief flags that the ₩ glyph crowds adjacent digits and asks for one consistent choice. ② I used `"KRW 184,500"` notation throughout (never the ₩ glyph). ③ Simple and unambiguous for a seller-facing money amount, and avoids introducing an extra gap-spacing style just to tame the glyph.

6. **Whether a destructive-action confirm dialog was needed.** ① The checklist actions (confirm deposits, submit dispute response, confirm identity, acknowledge policy) could be read as consequential enough to warrant a confirm step. ② I treated them as non-destructive affirmations (nothing is deleted or made irreversible from the user's perspective on this screen) and did not add a confirm dialog. ③ The DNA rule specifically scopes confirm dialogs to destructive actions; adding one here would be unearned friction with no real risk being guarded against.
