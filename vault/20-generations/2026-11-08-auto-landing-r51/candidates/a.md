# Candidate A — "Priority Duel"

Concept: a six-step fixed-sequence forced-choice duel over the 4-choose-2 = 6 pairs of repick's four matching criteria (Price, Condition grade, Seller rating, Fastest shipping) — each tap increments that criterion's win-count, a live weight bar chart re-derives and re-sorts from those counts after every pick, and a weighted-sum scoring function re-ranks six fixed dummy listings so the hero's "top match right now" card is a real, deterministic function of the accumulated weights, not a decorative shuffle.

## 브리프에 없던 것

- **Body-copy width math (brief's exact method, not `ch`, not estimated).** chars/line = containerPx ÷ (0.44 × fontSizePx). Target ~70 chars/line, with margin under the 75-char ceiling. At the page's 16px body size: 70 × 0.44 × 16 = 492.8px would sit right at the 70-char line — I used `BODY_MAX = "480px"` instead (480 ÷ (0.44×16) = 480 ÷ 7.04 ≈ 68.2 chars), applied via inline `maxWidth` on every paragraph (`DuelLanding.tsx`), to keep clear margin rather than "just barely" hitting 70.
- **Lime contrast — used exactly as given, not recomputed.** `#A3E635` text on `#0B0B0F` bg = 13.03:1 (used directly for all accent-colored text: eyebrows, the closing CTA's accent span, icon tints). `#0B0B0F` ink on `#A3E635` fill = 13.03:1 — every lime-filled surface (the match-score badge, both CTA buttons, the duel-progress dots) uses dark ink text, never white, per the brief's explicit reversal of the usual "white on fill" default for this one light accent hue.
- **Four criteria, not five — full round-robin, not a curated subset.** The brief allowed "4–5 criteria, 6–10 pairs" and either a full round-robin or a curated 5–6 subset. I picked exactly 4 criteria so the full round-robin (all 4-choose-2 = 6 pairs) is reachable in six duels with no curation decision to defend — every criterion appears in exactly 3 of the 6 duels, so no criterion is structurally favored by the fixed sequence itself. `DUEL_SEQUENCE` is hardcoded as `[[0,1],[2,3],[3,0],[1,2],[0,2],[1,3]]` — verified by hand to be the 6 unique pairs of {0,1,2,3} with none repeated.
- **Authenticity/verification kept out of the scoring weights on purpose.** Seller verification is shown as a trust badge on every card (it's one of the brief's required rich-card fields), but it is NOT one of the 4 duel-able criteria — it's binary and present on 5 of 6 listings, so weighting it would have made it a nearly-constant tie-breaker rather than a lever the duel meaningfully moves. Keeping the duel's 4 criteria as the only scored inputs keeps the "real derivation" demonstrably sensitive to every single pick.
- **Verified the scoring function actually produces different winners**, by hand, before committing to the dataset: with all weights equal (page load, no picks yet) the Leica M6 leads at 76.6 pts; weighting 100% toward Price flips the winner to the $58 ceramic vase set (cheapest item, 100 pts); weighting 100% toward Seller rating flips it to the vintage Rolex (highest-rated, 100 pts). This is why the six listings' price/condition/rating/shipping fields were chosen to spread widely rather than cluster — a narrow spread would have risked the same listing winning under every weight distribution, which would read as decorative rather than a real derivation.
- **Every position-dependent caption, not just the headline, reads from `deriveWeights`/`rankListings`.** Checked explicitly: the duel's "Pick N of 6" / "All 6 picks locked in" counter, the priority-bar percentages, the methodology disclosure's "`{criterion}` carries the most weight at `{pct}`%" line, the hero card's reasoning tags (`"Leads on Rating 96%"` — the percentage is the listing's own sub-score, recomputed per listing, per weight-change) and runner-up line, and the closing CTA's "Your top priority: `{criterion}`" headline plus its "`{listing}` leads the board at `{score}` pts" paragraph — all call `rankedCriteriaIndexes(weights)` / `rankListings(weights)` / `scoreListing(...)` directly, none are hand-typed strings for the starting state.
- **Image ids reused from already-shipped candidates in this same catalog** (r50/a's camera/sneaker/watch/vase/denim/headphones Unsplash ids) rather than picking six new unverifiable ones, specifically to reduce the broken-image risk the brief calls out — on top of the required fixed `aspect-ratio` + reserved background on every image container, and badges placed in a row below the image rather than overlaid on it.
- **Bar-chart motion stays transform-only.** The live weight bars animate via `scaleX` on a fixed-width track (not `width`), and the bars' reordering uses framer-motion's `layout` prop (FLIP, also transform-based) — both gated off entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion` (`layout={!reduce}`, fill transition duration 0) rather than just shortened.
