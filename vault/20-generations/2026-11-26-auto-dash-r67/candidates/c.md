# Candidate C — Ledgerflow

## Concept

Ledgerflow (`/dash-evolve/r67/c`) is a subscription billing and revenue console — MRR, churn, expansion, and plan-level movement for a B2B SaaS finance/revenue-ops team. The macro layout is the assigned hero + inline-stats shape, deliberately without one dominant chart: a hero card leads with the current MRR figure as large tabular-nums text plus a period-over-period delta, a 30D/90D/6M segmented-control period toggle, and a thin always-visible MRR sparkline, then four inline secondary stats below it (New MRR, Expansion MRR, Contraction MRR, Churned MRR), each printing its own dollar total as plain text next to its own small sparkline — none of the five charts in that card ever gates its headline number behind a hover. Beneath the hero, the page's small-multiples device is a grid of four compact per-plan-tier "revenue bridge" waterfalls (Starter/Growth/Scale/Enterprise) — floating bars from starting MRR through new, expansion, contraction and churn to ending MRR, each card self-scaled to its own min/max and each bar's dollar amount printed underneath it as permanent text. This was chosen specifically to avoid reproducing this catalog's recent shared-axis bullet-chart grid: a waterfall-per-segment device has no common ceiling to compare bars against, reads bridge-style rather than gauge-style, and its own layout code (`WaterfallChart` in `ui.tsx`) computes running-total bar positions rather than drawing a target-marker-on-a-track. Below that sits a sortable, filterable accounts table (`table-fixed` + `colgroup`, `aria-sort`, avatar cells via `next/image`, status/plan badges). Two selection axes are deliberately independent and live on different widgets: (A) clicking a plan's waterfall card toggles `pinnedPlanId`, which drives only the plan-detail panel beside the grid (a tabbed Bridge/Top-accounts breakdown with a progress bar for that plan's share of total MRR) — it never touches the table's rows, sort, or filters; (B) each account row's Trend-column sparkline carries its own fully local, keyboard-reachable hover/focus inspector (`InspectableSparkline`) that shows a per-week tooltip and is cleared on blur/mouse-leave — it never reads or writes `pinnedPlanId`. Four real interactions ship: the period toggle (recomputes only the hero cluster), the table's column sort + status/plan filters (segmented control + a checkbox popover), the click-to-pin plan selection, and the per-row trend hover/focus inspector; a ⌘K command palette (pin a plan, switch period, export) and a toast-confirmed "Export report" action round out the shell. Variety assignment: light theme (white canvas, `zinc-50`-wash main region), emerald accent used only for focus rings, active segmented/tab state, the primary CTA and positive deltas, and Pretendard-only throughout — including the "Ledgerflow" wordmark and the hero figure — with exactly three rendered weights route-wide (400/500/600; no `font-bold`, no bare bold UA defaults). Every secondary/muted text node sits on `zinc-600` or darker rather than `zinc-500`, satisfied uniformly on both the plain-white canvas and every tinted surface (segmented tracks, tab underlines, pills, badges), which exceeds the rule's own near-white/tinted split rather than relying on it. `MRR_WEEKLY` and all four hero stats are a cumulative sum/slice of four deterministic weekly movement arrays (no `Math.random`/`Date.now`/`new Date()`), and each plan's waterfall is that same company-wide movement total split by a fixed per-plan share, so every number on the page reconciles back to one dataset.

## 브리프에 없던 것

1. ① How many plan tiers and accounts to model, and what mechanism should generate the MRR trend line itself.
   ② Four plan tiers with a fixed revenue-share split (7/22/33/38%), and a company-wide `MRR_WEEKLY` series built as the running cumulative sum of four hand-authored weekly movement curves (new/expansion/contraction/churn) rather than a hand-typed trend line.
   ③ Arbitrary but engineered for internal consistency — deriving MRR cumulatively from the same four arrays the hero stats and the per-plan bridges both read means every number at every level of the page reconciles by construction, never a second series that merely "looks right."

2. ① Whether the period toggle should also resize the plan-bridge small-multiples grid, or leave it on its own fixed window.
   ② The period toggle (30D/90D/6M) only recomputes the hero card's own figures; the bridge grid always reports a fixed trailing-4-week ("this month") window regardless of the hero's selected period.
   ③ Deliberate, to keep the page's controls' blast radii narrow and non-overlapping — the brief explicitly prefers split, independently-scoped recompute over one shared global one, and tying the bridge grid to the hero's toggle would have coupled two otherwise-independent widgets.

3. ① How the small-multiples waterfall cards should be scaled against one another.
   ② Each plan's waterfall is scaled to its own min/max rather than sharing one ceiling across all four cards.
   ③ Standard convention for a small-multiples waterfall set (unlike a bullet-chart grid, which needs a shared axis to compare against a common target) — and it is also the single clearest thing that keeps this device from reading like the shared-axis bullet-chart grid the brief asked not to reproduce.

4. ① Whether the accounts table should be framed as "all" of the company's revenue or an illustrative sample.
   ② It is titled "Top accounts" and shows 13 named accounts summing to roughly 60% of the modeled company MRR — the remainder is implied long-tail volume, never claimed to be exhaustive.
   ③ Arbitrary but realistic: a billing console at this MRR scale would almost certainly paginate or sample its accounts table, and avoiding the word "all" sidesteps a false-precision claim against the macro MRR figure shown in the hero.

5. ① Whether the "ultra-wide cap" rule requires an actual numeric `max-w` somewhere on the page.
   ② No `max-w` is set anywhere; the 12-col grid and every card simply fill the width to the right of the sidebar at any viewport, including 1920px.
   ③ The rule only requires that a cap, if present, never bites before 1920px — omitting the cap entirely trivially satisfies that and avoids squeezing the table or the waterfall grid artificially on an ultra-wide monitor; this mirrors a documented choice already used elsewhere in this catalog for the same reasoning.

6. ① Which of the two grouping-control styles (pill vs. underline) to use for the page's two different switching jobs — filtering/period selection vs. tab navigation within a panel.
   ② A pill-style `SegmentedControl` for the hero's period toggle and the table's status filter; a visually distinct underline `Tabs` for the plan-detail panel's Bridge/Top-accounts switch.
   ③ Arbitrary but intentional — keeping the two control families visually distinct stops a viewer from conflating "pick one of several filters" with "switch which content is displayed," since both appear close together in the same layout region.
