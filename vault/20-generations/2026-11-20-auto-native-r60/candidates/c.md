# r60 / c — Dispute Evidence Checklist

One-line concept: a `FlatList` checklist of 5 evidence items (photos, receipt, chat log, packaging photo, listing screenshot) a buyer taps to mark attached/not-attached, with a bottom band that is absent at 0 attached and otherwise shows a live, derived "X of 5 attached — Submit available at 3" sentence (announced via `accessibilityLiveRegion="polite"` + `accessibilityRole="alert"`), gating a disabled-until-threshold Submit button.

## 브리프에 없던 것

1. **Exact evidence items and their copy.** The brief named the 3 categories ("photos, receipt, chat messages") and said "4-5 items." ① Decided on 5 items: photos of the item, original order receipt, chat messages with seller, photo of packaging/shipping label, and screenshot of the original listing. ② The extra two (packaging photo, listing screenshot) are realistic additional evidence a real marketplace dispute flow asks for — packaging proof speaks to shipping-damage vs. seller-misrepresentation, and a listing screenshot pins down what was actually promised at purchase time, which directly supports "item not as described." ③ Arbitrary but domain-grounded extrapolation from the 3 named categories to reach the "4-5" range the brief asked for.

2. **Which items start pre-attached, and how many.** The brief said "some pre-attached, some not" with no count. ① Started with 2 of 5 attached (photos, receipt), leaving 3 not attached (chat, packaging, listing screenshot). ② Chose the two most load-bearing / already-in-hand items (a buyer typically already has photos and the receipt before opening a dispute; chat history, packaging photos, and old listing screenshots take more digging) as the "already attached" set. ③ Arbitrary but consistent with how a real buyer would naturally progress through the flow — also lets the band's live count actually change when a reader taps a row, demonstrating the selection-derived form rather than starting maxed-out.

3. **The Submit threshold number and its wording.** The brief's example text ("3 of 5 attached — Submit available at 3") implies a minimum of 3 but never says that's fixed vs. illustrative. ① Took it as literal: `MIN_REQUIRED = 3` out of 5 total items, exported as a named constant in `data.ts`. ② The example string in the brief is reused verbatim as the target shape, just substituting the live totals. ③ Straightforward reading of the given example; a named constant (not a magic number inline) keeps it auditable and matches the "derived state" requirement — Submit's disabled state is `attachedCount < MIN_REQUIRED`, never a separate flag.

4. **Dispute case details (order id, item, reason, filed date).** The brief said "make up a realistic deterministic dispute reason" but gave no specific product/order. ① Invented: Order #RP-48213, "Sony WH-1000XM4 Wireless Headphones," reason "Item Not as Described," detail about scratches/cracked hinge vs. a "like new" listing claim, filed date written as the literal string "Filed Nov 18, 2026" (no `Date` object, no `Date.now()`). ② A mid-range electronics item is a common real dispute category and keeps the reason concrete enough that each evidence item (photos of scratches, receipt, listing screenshot showing "no scuffs" claim) ties back to it logically. ③ Arbitrary but internally consistent — every evidence item's description was written to plausibly support this specific reason rather than being generic filler.

5. **Checkbox visual (vector, not emoji/glyph).** The brief required a vector icon rather than an emoji for the attached state, but didn't specify a construction. ① Built a checkmark from two small rotated `View` rectangles (a "stem" and "kick" bar at ±45°) inside a rounded square box, rather than any Unicode glyph (e.g. "✓") or emoji. ② Chose this over a Unicode checkmark glyph to stay unambiguously inside "simple vector shapes," since the brief's no-emoji rule was paired with "use simple vector shapes/icons instead" and a hand-built two-rectangle check is the most literal reading of that. ③ Also means the `StatusChip` text ("Attached" / "Needed") still carries the meaning on its own, so color/icon/text are all paired per the no-color-only-meaning rule.

6. **Row-level `accessibilityHint` wording.** The brief banned hints describing behavior a Pressable doesn't implement, but didn't give exact copy for a toggle row. ① Used "Marks this evidence item as attached" / "...as not attached," worded to describe exactly and only the state flip `toggleItem` performs — no mention of opening a camera, file picker, or any upload. ② Chosen to be strictly truthful to the placeholder toggle's actual effect, per the explicit no-op-hint rule in the brief.

7. **Whether rows need any nested-interactive-child handling.** The brief flagged the `accessible={true}`-collapses-subtree bug as a recurring issue but noted it "only matters if a row has multiple independently-tappable children." ① Each evidence row has exactly one interactive target (the whole row toggles attached state) — no secondary "view attachment" sub-button was added. ② Decided the simplest, most literal reading of the "checklist item" requirement didn't call for a second interactive affordance per row, since the brief only asked for an attach/toggle state, not a detail/preview action; this sidesteps the collapse bug entirely rather than needing a workaround for it.

8. **Band visibility/copy when Submit is already enabled.** The brief's example sentence only showed the sub-threshold phrasing ("...Submit available at 3"). ① Once `attachedCount >= MIN_REQUIRED`, the band drops the "at {n}" clause and reads "X of 5 attached — Submit available" (no trailing number, since the condition is already met). ② Avoided an oddly redundant "Submit available at 3" after the threshold is already cleared; this is the natural completion of the brief's own pattern rather than a new one.
