# r59 / c — Seller Plan Comparison

One-line concept: a two-tab segmented switch ("Starter" / "Plus", `accessibilityRole="tab"`) drives a single piece of state that fully swaps which tier's perk detail renders in the list below — not a persistent side-by-side table — and a bottom chooser bar whose button label tracks the open tab live, then locks into a permanent "You're on Plus — manage anytime" status band once pressed.

## 브리프에 없던 것

1. **Which 6 perks, and their exact Starter/Plus values.** The brief gave examples ("listing limit, support response time, fee rate, payout speed, analytics access, featured placement") but no exact copy. ①Used exactly those 6, in that order, with: listing limit "25 active listings" vs "Unlimited active listings"; support response "48-hour email response" vs "4-hour priority chat"; fee rate "7.5% of sale price" vs "4.0% of sale price"; payout speed "5 business days after delivery" vs "Next business day after delivery"; analytics "7-day sales summary" vs "90-day trend and buyer demand data"; featured placement "Not included" vs "2 featured slots every month". ②All six example categories named in the assignment, nothing invented beyond them, numeric values deterministic literals in `data.ts`. ③Keeps the list short enough to read as one `FlatList` pass on a phone screen while still giving every row a concrete, legible number instead of vague qualitative text.

2. **Whether Plus can ever be the "weaker" tier on some row.** The brief only said "for perks where the selected tier is weaker" — it didn't fix whether that's symmetric. ①Made Plus strictly superior on every single row (a pure upsell structure), so the inline "Upgrade to unlock…" hint only ever appears while the Starter tab is open, never on Plus. ②Encoded as a single `isViewingStarter` check in the renderer rather than a per-row "which tier is weaker" flag. ③A pure upsell tier is the natural real-world shape for a "pick your plan" screen (nobody downgrades to see an upgrade-hint on the Plus tab), and it keeps the swap logic genuinely about *content*, not about computing a winner per row the way the membership baseline's cost-ranking engine does.

3. **Exact confirmed-band copy.** The brief gave the pattern ("You're on Plus — manage anytime" or the Starter equivalent) almost verbatim, but the pre-confirm helper line and the CTA's exact wording were open. ①Pre-confirm band shows "Review the perks above, then confirm when you're ready." plus a `Pressable` labeled `Choose ${tier.name}` (so "Choose Starter" / "Choose Plus", a derived string, not two hardcoded buttons swapped by visibility); post-confirm shows exactly `You're on ${tier.name} — manage anytime.` with `accessibilityRole="alert"` inside the always-mounted `accessibilityLiveRegion="polite"` chooser bar. ②`confirmedTierId` is a separate piece of state from `selectedTierId`, set once by `handleChoose` and never cleared, so the tabs stay live for browsing afterward but the band itself cannot be re-triggered into a "choose again" state. ③Matches the brief's required terminal-action-to-persistent-status form precisely, while keeping the label a true derived string so it can't drift out of sync with whichever tab is open at the moment of the tap.

4. **`accessibilityHint` on the Choose button.** Not specified by the brief. ①Used "Confirms this plan for your seller account. You can't switch back on this screen afterward." ②Matches `handleChoose`'s actual behavior exactly (it does set a permanent `confirmedTierId` and the band genuinely stops offering a choose action) — no promise of navigation or any effect the handler doesn't perform, per the no-op-hint rule.
