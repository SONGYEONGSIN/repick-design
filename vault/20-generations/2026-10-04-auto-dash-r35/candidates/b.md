**Fluxgate** is an edge-gateway traffic console whose hero is a single continuously-scrolling streaming area chart of requests-per-second across four edge regions, backed by a real pause/resume control and a persistent hero throughput KPI, with an inline stat strip above it and an independent, sortable/filterable incident log below.

## 브리프에 없던 것

1. **Tick rate and buffer length.** The brief asked for a deterministic sequence that "looks live" but didn't name an interval or a loop length. **Decision**: 1 point/second, drawn from a fixed 240-value buffer per region (so the pattern visibly repeats every 4 minutes if you watch long enough). **Why**: 1 Hz is the de facto convention for this chart family (matches the catalog's own "Real-Time Streaming" row, which cites 60–300s buffers) and 240s comfortably covers all three window options (60/90/180s) with room to spare, so no window size ever runs out of history.

2. **What "reduced motion" does to the pause/resume control itself**, beyond just not animating. The brief said render a "static, already-settled state" but didn't say whether the toggle should stay live. **Decision**: when `prefers-reduced-motion` is set, the interval never starts at all (not merely visually frozen), and the Pause/Resume button itself is disabled with an inline note explaining why, rather than left clickable but inert. **Why**: a clickable control that silently does nothing is worse than no control — disabling it with a stated reason is more honest about what the page can and can't do under that preference.

3. **Whether switching the region or window-length toggle should reset the crosshair scrubber back to "live."** Nothing in the brief addressed this interaction between the two toggles and the scrub control. **Decision**: changing the window length (60/90/180s) snaps the scrubber back to the live edge (since the old index may no longer be meaningful at a different width), but switching regions leaves the scrubber's position untouched. **Why**: a window-length change redefines what each index along the track means, so re-anchoring to "now" avoids a silently wrong reading; a region change doesn't change the track's meaning (same relative position, different series), so preserving the user's chosen vantage point felt more respectful of their intent.

4. **Domain and brand.** The brief left the product entirely open ("any domain, brand and specifics yours to invent"). **Decision**: an edge-CDN/API-gateway operations console (Fluxgate), with four regions, a throughput/latency/error-rate triad, and an incident log drawn from plausible gateway events (rate-limit spikes, node health-check failures, cert rotations). **Why**: edge/infra traffic is one of the clearest real-world cases where a streaming area chart is the natural hero rather than a stylistic choice, which keeps the chart's dominance in the layout justified by the domain rather than imposed on it.
