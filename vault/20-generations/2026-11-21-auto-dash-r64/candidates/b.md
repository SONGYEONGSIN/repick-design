# Candidate b — Jobline

Jobline is a field-service technician dispatch console for a six-person HVAC/plumbing/electrical crew ("Harlow Home Services"). The board/calendar is the page's main character: a `grid-cols-1 sm:grid-cols-2 lg:grid-cols-3` kanban of technician columns (today's jobs, each column a fixed-height `overflow-y-auto` panel, never a horizontally-scrolling board) toggles with a percentage-positioned day timeline (7:00 AM–6:00 PM, one row per technician, job bars placed by `left`/`width` math off fixed minute-of-day integers — no `<table>`, no `overflow-x-auto`, so there is never a second wide-scroll container alongside the jobs table below). A "Crew capacity vs. target" bullet-chart grid sits above the board: six bullets, one per technician, utilization-vs-daily-capacity-target with the assigned/target hours and percentage always rendered as persistent text beside the bar (never hover-gated) and a qualitative overtime band plus a target tick, both communicated through neutral zinc shading and shape rather than a second hue. Selection is genuinely two-axis and never a single threaded `selectedId`: (a) clicking a technician's column header (board) or row header (calendar) pins that technician, which opens one slide-over drawer (Overview/Job-history tabs, a progress bar, job counts) and touches nothing else — the board and calendar keep rendering every technician and every job exactly as before; (b) hovering or focusing a job card, a timeline bar, or a bullet row reveals an ephemeral, purely local inline expansion (full address on a job card; a one-line "next job" note on a bullet row; a shared-within-that-row detail line on the timeline) that never reads or writes `pinnedTechId`. Five real interactions ship: ① hover/focus inline inspectors (keyboard-reachable via `onFocus`/`onBlur`, never an absolutely-positioned per-item tooltip) on job cards, timeline bars, and bullets; ② two independent real filters — a status segmented control (All/Scheduled/Active/Completed) plus a technician `<select>` that both drive the board/calendar, and a second, fully separate status filter + free-text search + sortable `<table>` (`table-fixed` + `colgroup`, `aria-sort`, mobile card fallback) in the jobs list below; ③ the board↔calendar segmented-control toggle; ④ the two-axis pin/hover selection split above; ⑤ a ⌘K command palette (switch view, jump a status filter, pin a technician). Font: `--font-sans` (Pretendard) for all body/table text at 400/500/600; `--font-display-wide` (Archivo Display) only on the h1, section h2s, the drawer title, and the wordmark, also pinned to 600 — three rendered weights total, confirmed by grep across every file (`font-bold`/`font-light`/etc. never appear). Accent: green only (`green-600` for non-text fills/rings, `green-700/800` wherever text sits on or beside it) — no amber/blue/rose anywhere; "in progress" and "urgent" read through a solid dark-zinc fill and a triangle glyph instead of a second hue. Heading audit: exactly one `h1` ("Dispatch"), then `h2`s (bullet grid, schedule section, jobs table, and the two dialog titles when open) before any `h3` (board column / calendar row headers) — no skip. Completeness vs. reference level (Mercury/Monarch/n8n-grade): full app shell (sidebar + workspace switcher + bottom user menu, mobile drawer; topbar search/⌘K/primary action/notifications/avatar menu, all controls 44px), the full component system (Card, sortable table, real ARIA tabs, segmented controls, dropdown/popover ×4, badges, progress, sparkline, bullet grid), and a working "New job" modal that actually pins the assigned technician on submit rather than a decorative stub.

## 브리프에 없던 것

**① Product name and domain specifics**
① The brief assigned only the archetype ("field-service/technician dispatch console") and the bullet-chart requirement, not a product name, trade mix, technician roster, or job data.
② Invented "Jobline" (workspace: "Harlow Home Services") with six technicians across HVAC/plumbing/electrical and 31 hand-authored jobs spanning scheduled/in-progress/completed/cancelled, sized to land the bullet grid in the catalog's "ideal 3–10" range with a visibly varied utilization spread (52%–106%).
③ A dispatch console needs a believable multi-technician, multi-trade day to make the board, the bullets, and the timeline all read as the same real schedule rather than placeholder rows.

**② How literally to take "this is your only saturated color"**
① The reference pattern this candidate's shell was adapted from (a prior round's booking-calendar candidate) used three saturated hues at once — an orange brand accent plus a separate emerald "success" and an implicit red "error" state — which is a common and defensible "brand accent vs. semantic status colors" split, but this round's brief states the single accent is "your only saturated color," with no semantic-color carve-out mentioned.
② Took the stricter reading: every status/priority signal (in-progress, urgent, cancelled) is built from zinc neutrals and shape/weight (a solid dark fill, a dashed outline, a triangle glyph) instead of amber/blue, leaving green as the literal only saturated color anywhere on the page, including inside badges and timeline bars.
③ Given this round names "single-accent principle" as an explicit diversity constraint (unlike earlier rounds), erring toward the stricter literal reading seemed safer than reusing a multi-hue semantic-status convention the brief may have just tightened.

**③ Calendar view as a percentage-positioned timeline instead of a date grid**
① The brief's board/calendar toggle references a generic "day/week calendar view," which usually implies a month/week date grid (the pattern this console's shell borrowed from elsewhere), but this console has no multi-day data at all — it is explicitly a same-day dispatch view.
② Built the calendar view as a single day's hour-by-hour timeline (7a–6p), one row per technician, jobs placed by `(start-420)/660` percentage math — the same underlying per-technician job list the board renders, just laid out on a time axis instead of a stacked list.
③ A date grid would have forced inventing multi-day data the domain doesn't call for; a same-day timeline is the natural "calendar" reading for a dispatch console and keeps the view toggle meaningfully different from the board (list order vs. time position) without drifting toward a second unrelated feature.

**④ Where the pin lands: an overlay drawer, not a docked rail**
① The brief gives the drawer/KPI-card choice as an example ("e.g. a detail drawer or a KPI card") but doesn't fix which, and this round's diversity note explicitly warns against a persistent side rail competing with the board — exactly the master-detail shape another candidate this round already owns.
② Made the technician detail panel a slide-over overlay (`fixed`, dismissible, no permanent layout width) rather than a docked sidebar, so pinning never shrinks the board/calendar's own column budget.
③ This keeps the pin's effect genuinely confined to "one summary widget" in both substance (no recompute) and layout (no permanent space taken from the board), rather than quietly becoming a second master-detail layout under a different name.

**⑤ Bullet-chart scale and qualitative-band thresholds**
① The brief specifies the bullet chart's cardinality (3–10 per grid) and the always-visible-text rule, but not the track's numeric scale or where the qualitative bands should sit.
② Set a shared 600-minute (10h) scale across every bullet so all six technicians plot on one consistent axis regardless of their own capacity target (480 or 360 min), with the "overtime band" starting at 125% of each technician's own target rather than a fixed minute value.
③ A shared scale is what makes six side-by-side bullets comparable at a glance (per-bullet auto-scaling would make a 52%-utilized tech's bar look identical in length to a 106%-utilized one on a different scale); 125%-of-target is a believable "comfortable overrun ceiling" for a trade where some overtime is normal.
