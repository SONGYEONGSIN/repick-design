Offer Comparison: a seller reviews 5 simultaneous buyer offers on one listing side by side, sorts them by recency or price, and accepts exactly one through an inline per-card Cancel/Confirm step that implicitly declines the rest and leaves every card visibly marked Accepted/Declined afterward.

RENDER_CHECK: Compare offers

Export: `OfferComparisonScreen` (named export, also re-exported as default) — `native/src/evolve/r16/a/OfferComparisonScreen.tsx`. Data: `native/src/evolve/r16/a/data.ts`.

## 브리프에 없던 것

1. ① Which listing/photo/price to show as the item being sold, and how many competing offers. ② A pair of Nike sneakers (₩85,000 asking) with 5 offers spread across, below, at, and above asking (₩78k–₩92k) and across three time bands (2h/5h ago, "Yesterday" ×2, "2 days ago"). ③ Arbitrary but deliberately varied per the brief's "vary it" instruction — 5 is the top of the suggested 3-5 range so the sort interaction has something to prove, and the spread exercises the below/at/above-asking comparison label on every card at least once.

2. ① What the one real sort/highlight interaction should be and how it's exposed. ② Two chips, "Most recent" (default) and "Highest price," that actually re-sort the FlatList's `data` array (not a decorative toggle). ③ Chose sort over a separate "highlight" affordance because the brief already treats price as the natural axis for a comparison screen, and a second orthogonal highlight mechanism would risk looking decorative next to it — one real interaction beats two half-real ones.

3. ① Where the destructive confirmation lives, since this screen has no single global action (each offer needs its own accept control) unlike `payout`'s single bottom band. ② Converted the *specific offer's* Accept button into an inline Cancel/Confirm row inside that card (not a global bottom band), wrapped in its own `accessibilityLiveRegion="polite"` container with the confirmation sentence on `accessibilityRole="alert"`. ③ Directly follows §3's instruction to convert "the relevant control/band itself" — for a per-entity destructive pick among several, the relevant control is the row being acted on, not a screen-wide band; `payout`'s pattern (global band) doesn't transfer because payout only ever has one pending action, this screen has up to five.

4. ① What happens to the *other* offers' Accept buttons while one card is mid-confirmation, since two simultaneous confirms would be an inconsistent state. ② Disabled (grayed, `accessibilityState={{disabled:true}}`, no hint) on every other card until the pending confirm is resolved (confirmed or canceled). ③ Arbitrary but necessary to keep the state machine single-threaded and honest — an app that let you start accepting a second offer mid-confirmation would produce an undefined resulting state, which the brief explicitly forbids ("no dead-end taps").

5. ① Whether sorting should stay enabled after a decision is final. ② Disabled once `stage === "resolved"` (chips grey out, `accessibilityState.disabled` set). ③ Arbitrary product judgment: once one offer is Accepted and the rest Declined, reordering serves no purpose and re-enabling it would imply the record is still live/reversible, contradicting the "irreversible" requirement.

6. ① How to phrase and place the final resolved state so it's a real result, not a dead end. ② A dedicated banner above the sort row (bordered in accent, text on `accessibilityRole="alert"` inside its own live region) stating who was accepted and how many were declined, plus a persistent per-card "✓ Accepted" / "– Declined" pill (text+glyph, not color alone) on every card. ③ Directly required by the brief ("show the real resulting state … no dead-end taps") and by the a11y rule pairing color with text/icon; the pill glyphs (✓ / –) are plain characters, not emoji, per the no-emoji rule.

7. ① Whether to use `KRW` Latin notation or the ₩ glyph, and how to avoid the ₩-into-digit collision named in the brief. ② Kept ₩ with a literal space before the digits via a shared `formatWon()` helper (mirrors `native/src/payout/data.ts`'s existing convention). ③ Consistency with an existing screen's already-settled resolution of the same named tradeoff, rather than inventing a second convention for the same problem in the same codebase.

8. ① Whether the list needs `FlatList` virtualization given only 5 items, and how to reconcile that with a header block (listing summary, sort chips, resolved banner) above the list. ② Used `FlatList` with `ListHeaderComponent` rather than a `ScrollView`+`.map()` or a nested `FlatList`-in-`ScrollView`. ③ The brief prefers `FlatList` "for anything list-shaped and reorderable" — sorting reorders these cards, which tips it past the "short fixed list, mapping is acceptable" carve-out — and `ListHeaderComponent` is the standard RN idiom for header content that must scroll with a reorderable list without double-scroll-container bugs.
