# Candidate b — "Tipping the Scale"

Two fixed, named listings of the same camera — a Sony α7 III body — are compared head to head: Listing A, sold by **FocalPoint Studio** ($1,050, Good condition, ships in 1 day, 81/100 trust · 140 sales), against Listing B, sold by **Aperture Collective** ($1,340, Like New, ships in 4 days, 97/100 trust · 410 sales). Four attribute scores are derived once and never change: `priceScore = round(100 × minPrice ÷ price)` (A=100, B=78), `conditionScore` from a fixed grade rubric (A=72, B=96), `shipScore = clamp(100 − (days−1)×15, 0, 100)` (A=100, B=55), and `trustScore` given directly (A=81, B=97). The visitor's four weight sliders (Price/Condition/Ship-speed/Seller-trust priority, 0–100 each) feed `weightedScore = Σ(weight × attributeScore) ÷ Σweight`, computed live for both listings. Default weights `{price:70, condition:30, ship:55, trust:20}` (and the same values on every reset) produce A=93.0 vs B=76.0 — a clear 17.0-point lead for Listing A, never a level default. The score difference drives the beam: `tiltDeg = clamp((scoreRight − scoreLeft) × 0.5, −16, 16)`, rendered as a literal two-pan SVG balance (beam + two drop-pans rotating as one rigid group around a static fulcrum, via `framer-motion`'s `motion.g` with `rotate` and a pixel `transformOrigin` matching the SVG's own coordinate space), giving −8.5° at default (left pan down). Accent is green-700 `#15803d`: **5.02:1** against white text/background, **3.96:1** against dark-ink (zinc-950) text — white is used for all small text on the filled accent (clears 4.5:1), and the accent itself is used directly for small accent-colored text/icons/focus rings on the white page background (it already clears 4.5:1 there, so no separate tint color was needed). The causal chain reaches three surfaces: each listing card's own "Weighted score" + expandable arithmetic, the beam tip + text leader-caption, and the closing-CTA recap line + button, which names the current leader, the exact score gap, and which weight is currently heaviest/lightest.

## Brief gaps

- **Decide:** whether the two listings needed real photos (via `next/image`) or a generated placeholder, given the explicit "no random image service" risk and this repo having no `next.config` image-domain allowlist to check against.
  **Decided:** a plain inline-SVG/icon placeholder (a centered Camera glyph in a flat tinted circle, aspect-ratio reserved, badges in a row below — never overlaid), mirroring the existing `CategoryArt.tsx` convention in this catalog.
  **Why:** fully sidesteps the remote-host risk and the `next/image`-without-`unoptimized` constraint, keeps both cards visually identical so the comparison reads as apples-to-apples, and matches a pattern this codebase already uses for exactly this reason.

- **Decide:** how literally to implement "beam tips toward the winner" so it reads as a real two-pan balance and not a disguised single-needle gauge, while still only needing one clamped scalar (`tiltDeg`).
  **Decided:** a rigid rotating group (beam line + two vertical drops + two pan plates) around a *static* fulcrum pin/stand drawn outside the group, plus two faint hand-computed travel-limit ticks (sin/cos at ±16°, rounded to 2 decimals: `(241.32, 96.18)` / `(241.32, 43.82)`) showing the clamp visually.
  **Why:** two pans + one beam + one fulcrum is the literal device the brief requires; keeping the stand/fulcrum static (only the beam+pans rotate) is what makes it read as a scale instead of a spinning gauge, and the ticks give the clamp a visible, honest "this is as far as it goes" cue instead of a hidden limit.

- **Decide:** whether the Product Preview section (section 2) should just repeat the Hero's two cards, or do something structurally different while still being genuinely "rich" and still reinforcing (not duplicating) the live chain.
  **Decided:** a semantic `<table>` row-comparing the four fixed attributes (with per-row "Favors A/B" text, never color-only) plus one final live "Weighted score" row explicitly captioned "updates with your weights" — wrapped in `overflow-x-auto` so it never forces page-level horizontal scroll at 390px.
  **Why:** avoids the overclaim trap (only the last row is actually live, and the copy says so), gives the comparison a second, textual surface distinct from the beam/cards, and the `overflow-x-auto` wrapper was the one deliberate mobile-safety call for the single element on this page genuinely wide enough to risk clipping.
