# Candidate a — Group Buy Roster

Built `GroupBuyRosterScreen` (`native/src/evolve/r43/a/GroupBuyRosterScreen.tsx` + `data.ts` + `ContributorRow.tsx`): a Group Buy / Split Purchase Roster for one fixed dummy item ("Leica M6 Camera Body, Black", target ₩ 1,850,000). A sticky header — never wrapped in the FlatList, a plain sibling `View` sitting above it — shows the item thumbnail/title/subtitle plus a big tabular-nums readout ("₩ X of ₩ Y pooled"), a progress-fill bar, and a one-line meta ("₩ Z still needed · N of M contributors have pledged"). Below it, a `FlatList` renders six contributor rows: five fixed, already-pledged amounts (Mina ₩450,000, Carlos ₩350,000, Priya ₩200,000, Dev ₩150,000, Jordan ₩0/"Invited — hasn't pledged yet") plus one live row, "You," the only state this screen mutates — a 44×44pt +/- stepper in ₩50,000 steps, clamped [₩0, ₩1,000,000], starting at ₩400,000 (total ₩1,550,000, ₩300,000 short of target; six taps of "+" land exactly on target).

The band mechanic assigned this round is a sum-threshold derived gate: `const poolReady = totalPooled >= ITEM.targetPrice;`, recomputed every render from `FIXED_OTHERS_TOTAL + youAmount`. This generalizes the catalog's existing "derived boolean gates a band" family (selected-count>0 in r16/b, dirty-vs-saved diff in r23/a, nullable-selection-id in r42/c) to a numeric sum comparison rather than a count, a diff, or a nullability check. While `poolReady` is false the band is a plain non-`Pressable` `View` ("poolBandInert") stating the exact shortfall in a sentence — never a disabled-looking button inviting a tap that does nothing. The instant the sum crosses the target it becomes a real enabled `Pressable` ("poolBandGo", "Lock In Purchase"). Because the stepper can move either direction pre-lock, the gate can also close again (sum drops back under target), and the band reverts to inert — this round's mechanic is explicitly reversible until the user actually taps "Lock In Purchase," which flips a third, terminal `lockedIn` state ("poolBandLocked") and disables the "You" stepper. All three band variants (`poolBandInert`/`poolBandGo`/`poolBandLocked` plus their `*Title`/`*Hint` siblings) are fresh style-key names — the forbidden `bandBlocked`/`bandReady`/`bandDone` family used verbatim by six other screens is not reused anywhere in this file, and the control flow is also structurally simpler than that family (no step-array, no `jumpTo`/accordion-expand, since there's nothing to navigate to — just one number crossing one line).

Accessibility follows the two-pattern split from this round's brief exactly. The header's big "₩ X of ₩ Y pooled" readout is the "re-derived every render" pattern — plain text, no live region, nothing to clear, since it's never stale. The band's outer wrapper carries `accessibilityLiveRegion="polite"` unconditionally (the only live region on the screen); inside it, a separate transient line is *mounted only when there is something to say* (`{liveMessage ? <Text accessibilityRole="alert">...} : null}`), fired by a `useEffect` keyed on `poolReady` that explicitly skips its first run via a `didMountRef` guard, so nothing is announced on initial mount even if the pool happened to start ready. The message clears itself via `setTimeout` (4s) with cleanup on unmount, matching the twice-reproduced codebase rule. The "You" stepper's `accessibilityHint` tells the truth in both states — "Raises/lowers your pledge by fifty thousand won" pre-lock, "Your pledge is locked now that the purchase has been locked in" post-lock — never promising a decrease/increase that the disabled handler won't perform. No wrapper carries `accessible={true}`; the decorative avatar-initial `View` is hidden from the tree via `importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"` instead of collapsing the row (which nests two independent stepper `Pressable`s and would make them unreachable).

## Brief gaps

- Decision: what "Lock In Purchase" actually does, since the brief only specifies the gate becoming "a real, enabled primary action," not a backend. Decided: a purely local state flip (`lockedIn`) that disables further pledge edits and swaps the band into a terminal, non-interactive "Purchase locked in" display, with no navigation and no fake async call. Why: mirrors the existing-codebase convention for terminal actions on screens with no real network side effect (seen in `r42/c`'s "Confirm Plan" and in `SellerVerificationScreen`'s "Submit for review"), and avoids an `accessibilityHint` that would promise a screen transition that doesn't exist.
- Decision: whether the sum-threshold gate should be a strict `>` or a `>=` comparison, and whether it should be reversible before lock-in. Decided: `>=` ("reaches OR exceeds," per the brief's own wording) and fully reversible pre-lock — the stepper can move the sum back under target and the band reverts to inert, announcing that reversal through the same live-message slot. Why: the brief explicitly says "reaches or exceeds," and treating the gate as irreversible-once-crossed (only resettable by a separate undo) would have invented a second mechanic (a confirm/undo pair) the brief didn't ask for; a pure derived boolean that can flip either way until an explicit terminal action is the simplest reading consistent with the "derived boolean gates a band" family's other members, all of which are freely reversible until their own terminal step.
- Decision: who the one editable contributor is, and how large to make their adjustable range relative to the target. Decided: a single "You" row (the viewer), ₩50,000 steps across [₩0, ₩1,000,000], with the five fixed contributors summing to ₩1,150,000 against a ₩1,850,000 target — chosen so the gate starts inert (₩300,000 short) and reaches exactly on the sixth tap, demonstrating both the inert→ready and ready→inert transitions without an impractical number of taps. Why: arbitrary, but constrained so every number divides cleanly (no rounding remainder when summing) since this is a financial screen where a visible off-by-one won't read as intentional design.
- Decision: whether to vary contributor-avatar fill color per row (the swatch tokens exist for exactly this kind of index-cycled decoration elsewhere in the catalog). Decided: a single flat `accentBg`/`accent` pairing for every avatar instead of cycling `swatch1`/`swatch2`/`swatch3` as a background behind the initial letter. Why: the `swatch*` tokens are documented in `tokens.ts` as light-to-mid-gray "photo-placeholder" swatches with no accompanying "text-safe" foreground color, and checking contrast by hand showed white-on-`swatch1` and ink-on-`swatch3` both fail AA at the 15px size these initials render at — rather than hardcode a third never-reused color just to pair safely with each swatch, I kept the one `accent`-on-`accentBg` combination this codebase already uses elsewhere for text-bearing chips (e.g. `editButtonText`/`editButton` in `MinimumOfferThresholdScreen`), which is contrast-safe by construction.
