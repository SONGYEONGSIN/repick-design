# Candidate a — "The Proof Ladder"

## Concept

**A five-rung, single-select verification-tier radiogroup for sellers, where climbing one rung live-recomputes estimated days-to-sell, buyer-reach percentage, and Repick's service fee, all threaded through a comparison strip and into the closing CTA's own button label.**

Repick is an AI-graded resale marketplace. Its one real lever that a seller actually controls before listing is *how much verification depth they opt into* — a plain photo-based self-description, versus Repick's AI visually grading the item, versus that AI grade also being spec-checked against an authenticity index, versus a human grader confirming the AI's call, versus a partner lab physically authenticating the item. That is a genuinely ordered, discrete ladder (five rungs, not a continuum, not independent toggles) — condition grading itself is already this catalog's most-mined angle (slope charts, duel comparators, stepper payout simulators all key off "grade"), but *choosing how deep the verification goes* is a different decision a seller makes exactly once per listing, before grading even happens, so it earns its own device rather than reusing condition-grade as the input.

**The device.** `Ladder.tsx` renders `role="radiogroup"` with five `role="radio"` buttons, one per tier, stacked top-to-bottom with the *highest* tier at the top (visually "climbing"). Roving `tabIndex` (0 on the selected rung, −1 on the rest), `aria-checked`, full keyboard support: ArrowUp/ArrowRight move focus+selection up one rung, ArrowDown/ArrowLeft down one, Home jumps to the top (highest) rung, End to the bottom (baseline) rung — and every arrow/Home/End key immediately changes the selection, not just focus, matching native radiogroup behavior. There is no drag, no continuous value, no +/- stepper and no independent on/off switches — exactly one rung is ever selected, by a discrete click or keypress on that exact rung. A single thin vertical rail (one `div`, one color, `aria-hidden`) connects the rungs — explicitly not a blueprint grid or line-art illustration (both banned by the brief): it is one plain line, nothing decorative layered on it.

**The output half (deliberately not reusing a banned/saturated visualization).** Three numbers come off the selected tier: `daysToSell`, `reach` (% of the active buyer pool whose minimum-verification filter the tier clears), and `fee` (Repick's cut for the verification work). The hero shows `daysToSell` as the primary oversized stat (inside a bordered callout card), and `reach` as a horizontal coverage bar in `ComparisonStrip.tsx` with a tick mark for *every* tier (not just the selected one) so the bar reads as "where this tier sits among all five," not a bare percentage. This is deliberately **not** a radial gauge, not a slope/bridge/waterfall chart, not a packed-bubble cluster, not a scatter plot and not a histogram — the five things the brief explicitly flagged as already-used output shapes in this catalog. A plain segmented horizontal bar-with-ticks was, as far as I could find (see the grep log below), unused as a *tier-comparison* visualization specifically. Two worded delta chips sit under the bar — "+N pts reach vs. {baseline}" and (when not already at the baseline) "+N pts vs. {tier directly below}" — so the comparison is legible in words and digits, never color alone.

**Threading to the close.** `LandingPage.tsx` holds the one piece of state (`selectedIndex`) and derives everything else through one pure function, `computeComparison(index)` in `ladder-math.ts` — the hero, the value section, and the closing CTA all consume the same derived object, so nothing can drift out of sync. The closing CTA's headline reads `"{tier.name} listings sell in about {days} days."` and its **button label itself** reads `"List at {tier.name} — ~{days}d to sell"` — the live number is not just in body copy, it is literally the clickable control's text, which is the brief's explicit hard requirement ("a page whose premise is 'your choice changes everything' must not go dead at the finish line"). The hero's own primary CTA also cites the live tier name as a bonus (not strictly required, only the closing CTA is mandated to).

**Catalog-overlap check (performed before finalizing).** Grepped `vault/20-generations/*-auto-landing-r*/candidates/*.md` and `vault/00-principles/landing-deltas-provisional.jsonl` for `ladder|tier|rung|ranked|ordered` (137 file hits, ~10 jsonl line hits). The jsonl hits and most file hits were either unrelated uses of the word ("ranked" in judge panel rankings, "tier" in pricing-plan contexts for dashboard/native rounds) or, in two native-round candidates (`r14-native/candidates/b.md`, `r1-developers/candidates/b.md`), an actual "ladder" *slider* device for an unrelated page type (gamified milestone progress / monthly-document-volume pricing) — neither a landing-type page, neither a single-select radiogroup, and neither sharing this input×output pair. Read `DECISION.md` for the two most recent landing rounds directly: r36/a used a condition-grade **slider** (continuous drag) driving a slope chart — different input mechanism (slider, not discrete radiogroup) and different output (slope chart, which I avoided). r37's three devices (typed-search combobox, 4-step click-any-order verification chain, drag-magnifier) share no input or output with this one. No landing-type round has used a vertical single-select ordered-tier radiogroup before this one.

## 브리프에 없던 것

1. **① Which domain concept the five rungs represent** (brief said "condition grades, or trade-in tiers, or membership/verification tiers — your call"). **② Decided**: seller-facing *verification depth* tiers (Seller Photos Only → AI Visual Grade → AI Grade + Spec Check → AI Grade + Human Review → Full Authentication). **③ Why**: condition grading itself is this catalog's most-reused angle (sliders, steppers, duel comparators already key off "grade" — see r33/a, r34/a, r35/c, r36/a, r37/a); verification *depth* is a decision a seller makes once, before any grade is even assigned, so it is both domain-plausible for an AI-graded marketplace and structurally distinct from every prior device.

2. **① The output metrics** (brief required "a primary number... payout/time/trust-score, your call" + a comparison + CTA threading). **② Decided**: estimated days-to-sell (primary), buyer-reach % (comparison strip), service fee % (value-section third column) — explicitly **not** a 0–100 "Trust Score," which r37's own judge panel flagged as "the single most-reused output type in the catalog (8+ prior instances)." **③ Why**: avoiding further saturation of that one output type per the brief's own diversity-axis note; days-to-sell and buyer-reach are both concrete, falsifiable-sounding numbers rather than an abstract composite score.

3. **① The output visualization shape.** **② Decided**: a plain horizontal bar with a tick mark per tier plus worded delta chips — not a gauge/slope/bridge/bubble/scatter/histogram (all explicitly banned as already-used). **③ Why**: direct compliance with the brief's banned-list; a horizontal "where does this tier sit among all five" bar with ticks was the simplest shape I could not find prior art for in the archive grep.

4. **① Exact numeric values for reach/days/fee per tier** (brief gave no numbers). **② Decided**: reach 34/58/76/89/97%, days 21/12/8/5/3, fee 0/0/1.5/2.5/4% — monotonic, hand-authored, fixed constants in `ladder-math.ts`, never `Math.random`. **③ Why**: monotonicity makes the "climbing helps" premise legible at every rung (no regression to explain away), and the fee only rising from the mid-tier up models a believable real cost (deeper authentication genuinely costs more to deliver) rather than every tier being strictly free-and-better, which would read as hype rather than the brief's required "trustworthy minimal tone."

5. **① Theme choice** (dark vs. light — brief said either is valid, "lean light OR confident dark," noting dark is slightly more saturated catalog-wide at 36:29). **② Decided**: light theme. **③ Why**: the two most recent landing rounds split 3-light/0-dark (r36) then went fully dark (r37) with emerald; picking light here keeps the short-run alternation going rather than defaulting to dark again, and stacks a confidently-executed light page against this round's likely dark siblings for genuine visual variety within the round.

6. **① Accent hue** (brief explicitly asked for a hue outside amber/teal/violet/blue, noting those — plus emerald, used twice in the immediately preceding two rounds — are saturated). **② Decided**: rose (`#E11D48`, Tailwind rose-600 family). **③ Why**: not in the banned/saturated list, and distinct from both of the last two rounds' accents (teal/emerald/blue in r36, emerald in r37). **Mandatory contrast computation** (manual sRGB relative-luminance arithmetic, formula `L=0.2126R+0.7152G+0.0722B` per-channel-linearized, contrast `(L1+0.05)/(L2+0.05)`):
   - `#E11D48` vs white `#FFFFFF`: **4.70:1** — clears the 4.5:1 *normal-text* AA floor (not just the 3:1 large-text floor), which is why rose is used **unlifted** for small accent text/eyebrows directly on this page's near-white backgrounds, unlike the brief's documented default violet (`#6E56CF`, 3.73:1 vs dark bg, which only clears the large-text floor and needs a lighter derived tint for small text).
   - `#E11D48` vs this page's dark ink `#15151A`: **3.87:1** — clears the 3:1 large-text/non-text floor but not the 4.5:1 body-text floor, so (mirroring the brief's own treatment of the default accent) I never place dark-ink text *on* an accent fill, and never use raw accent-on-dark-ink for small text; the accent is only used as a fill (with white text, see below) or as text/border directly on light surfaces.
   - White text on `#E11D48` fill (buttons): same **4.70:1** computation — passes AA for all text sizes, used for every filled CTA button.
   - `accentSoft` (`#FFE4E6`) background with `accentDeep` (`#9F1239`) text (selected-rung highlight, "AI match" badge, value-section stat labels): computed **6.68:1** — comfortably clears 4.5:1.
   - `accentDeep` vs white card background (stat-callout labels): computed **8.01:1**.
   - `zinc-500` (`#71717A`, `mutedOnBg`) vs white: computed **4.83:1** — passes the brief's "zinc-500 floor on near-white surfaces" rule with margin, not just eyeballed.
   - `zinc-600` (`#52525B`, `mutedOnSurface`) vs `zinc-100` (`#F4F4F5`, `surface`): computed **7.05:1** — passes the brief's "zinc-600 floor on tinted surfaces/pills" rule with margin.
   - Ghost/decorative numeral ("05" in the hero, `aria-hidden`): first tried `surfaceLine` (`#E4E4E7`) at large size — computed only **1.27:1** against white, and even `zinc-400` (`#A1A1AA`, the brief's own base "muted" token) only reaches **2.57:1** against pure white, both failing the 3:1 large-text floor the brief mandates for rendered ghost numerals. Corrected to `mutedOnBg` (zinc-500, **4.83:1**) instead — the brief's own warning that Lighthouse scans `aria-hidden` large text, and that "relying on faintness" is not a valid escape, is exactly the trap this caught.
   - Closing-CTA dark inversion panel (a single contrast band on an otherwise light page — see item 7): `accentTintOnDark` (`#FFB4C0`) vs panel bg `#15151A`: **10.88:1**; `mutedOnDark` (`#C9C9D1`) vs `#15151A`: **11.06:1**. Both far above the 4.5:1 floor.

7. **① Whether the closing CTA could deviate from the page's light theme** (brief doesn't address partial inversions). **② Decided**: yes — the closing CTA is a single dark (`fg`-colored) panel on the otherwise light page, a common light-theme editorial device (one contrast band, not a theme switch). **③ Why**: Stripe/Linear-style landing pages routinely close on a dark band for emphasis; since this introduces new color roles I computed their contrast ratios explicitly (see item 6) rather than treating the inversion as exempt from the brief's contrast rules.

8. **① Display typeface** (brief offers grotesk/wide/mono or none, and flags that the two immediately preceding rounds both used `wide`). **② Decided**: `--font-display-mono` (JetBrains Mono Display), applied only via the safe inline `style={{ fontFamily: 'var(--font-display-mono)' }}` form (never the broken bare `font-[var(...)]` bracket class — grepped the whole folder for `font-display|fontFamily` to confirm every instance uses the inline form). **③ Why**: avoids a third consecutive `wide` round per the brief's note; mono's ledger/dossier connotation fits a verification-tier device thematically (tabular, certificate-like) better than grotesk or wide would.

9. **① Body/line-length arithmetic** (brief mandates the 0.44em/char constant, forbids `ch` units, and requires showing the arithmetic). **② Decided**: all body paragraphs use `max-w-[490px]` at `text-[16px]`. **③ Arithmetic**: `chars-per-line = container-width-px ÷ (0.44 × font-size-px) = 490 ÷ (0.44 × 16) = 490 ÷ 7.04 ≈ 69.6 characters` — within the ≤75 ceiling and close to the ~70 target, applied uniformly across the hero subhead, value-section intro, product-preview intro, and closing-CTA body paragraph so line length doesn't drift between sections.

10. **① Exactly which three font weights** (brief mandates exactly 3, machine-measured on rendered weight). **② Decided**: 400 (`font-normal`), 600 (`font-semibold`), 800 (`font-extrabold`) — grepped the whole folder for `font-(thin|extralight|light|medium|bold|black)` after first drafting and caught one stray `font-bold` (700) on the Ladder's rung-index badge, corrected to `font-extrabold`; a second grep confirmed zero remaining out-of-set weight classes. **③ Why**: matches this repo's own established convention (seen verbatim in the prior `r37/a` candidate I read for context) rather than inventing a new triad.

11. **① Whether the Ladder rungs' accessible name should include the decorative index number / rung day-badge text** (brief requires content-IS-the-name, no competing `aria-label`, but says nothing about incidental sibling text inside the same control). **② Decided**: marked the circular index/check glyph `aria-hidden="true"` (purely decorative ordinal marker) and replaced the visible abbreviated day badge ("21d") with a paired `aria-hidden` visible badge + a separate `sr-only` span reading "21 days to sell" for the accessible name, so each rung's computed name reads cleanly as e.g. "Seller Photos Only, you describe the condition yourself... , 21 days to sell" rather than being polluted by a bare leading digit or an ambiguous abbreviation. **③ Why**: this is exactly the kind of incidental-content accessible-name noise the brief's `label-content-name-mismatch` warning is adjacent to (not the same failure mode, but the same family of "what actually gets read aloud" bug) — fixing it is a deliberate craft choice beyond the letter of the rule, done preemptively rather than left for the gate to catch.

12. **① Framer-motion animation targets for the comparison bar** (brief bans animating `width`/`height`/`top`/`left`, requires transform/opacity only). **② Decided**: the reach bar animates via `transform: scaleX` from a `transform-origin: left` full-width element, never via an animated `width` percentage. **③ Why**: caught on self-review — my first draft animated `width` directly, which is explicitly the banned pattern; corrected before finalizing, noted here since it's exactly the kind of mistake the brief calls out by name.
